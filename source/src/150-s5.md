## Revenue {#s5}

> **DEFINITIONS.**\
> **Total revenue**: price multiplied by quantity sold.\
> **Average revenue**: revenue per unit, which is the price, so the average revenue curve and the demand curve facing the firm are the same line.\
> **Marginal revenue**: the addition to total revenue from selling one more unit.\
> **Price taker**: a firm too small to move the market price, for which marginal revenue, average revenue and price are all equal.

A firm large enough to affect its own price faces a downward-sloping demand curve. Write it with price as the subject, $P(Q) = a - bQ$: the price the firm can charge if it wants to sell $Q$ units. Here $a$ is the price at which sales would dwindle to nothing and $b$ is the number of pounds the firm must knock off the price to sell one more unit.

$$TR(Q) = P(Q)\, Q = aQ - bQ^{2},\quad\quad AR = \frac{TR}{Q} = P(Q)\quad(\#e14)$$

Total revenue is the price multiplied by the number of units sold, and dividing it by quantity returns the price, so average revenue and demand are the same line. Differentiating $TR$:

$$MR = \frac{dTR}{dQ} = a - 2bQ\quad(\#e15)$$

Marginal revenue starts at the same height as demand and falls twice as steeply, so it hits the horizontal axis at exactly half the quantity that demand does. The factor of two has an economic reason: selling one more unit earns the firm the price of that unit, but to sell it the firm had to cut the price on every unit it was already selling. Applying the product rule to $TR = P(Q) \times Q$, rather than to the expanded quadratic, separates the two effects:

$$MR = P + Q\frac{dP}{dQ} = P\left( 1 + \frac{Q}{P} \cdot \frac{dP}{dQ} \right) = P\left( 1 + \frac{1}{PED} \right)\quad(\#e16)$$

The first term $P$ is the money the new unit brings in. The second, $Q\, dP/dQ$, is the money lost on the units already being sold: $dP/dQ$ is the price cut needed to shift one more, and $Q$ is how many units suffer it. Factorising out $P$ turns the bracket into an expression in the price elasticity of demand defined in section {sec:s1}, since $PED = (dQ/dP)(P/Q)$ is the reciprocal of $(Q/P)(dP/dQ)$.

$MR = 0$ exactly when $|PED| = 1$, which is where total revenue peaks. A firm with any price-setting power will therefore never knowingly operate where demand is inelastic, because cutting output there would raise revenue and cut costs at the same time.

A firm in perfect competition is too small to move the market price, so $dP/dQ = 0$, and ({eq:e16}) collapses to $MR = P = AR$: a horizontal line at the market price.

> **ASSUMPTIONS.**\
> The firm charges one price to every buyer, so cutting the price to sell one more unit means cutting it on all the others. Price discrimination, in section {sec:s12}, is what happens when that assumption is dropped.\
> The demand curve facing the firm is known and straight, which is what makes the factor of two in marginal revenue exact rather than approximate.
