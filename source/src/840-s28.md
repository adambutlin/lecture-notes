## Back to the frontier {#s28}

Section {sec:s15} drew the production possibility frontier and said two things about it: everything inside is waste, and everything outside is impossible this year.

Demand-side policy, in sections {sec:s17} to {sec:s23}, moves the economy towards the boundary or away from it. A negative output gap is a point inside the frontier: the workers and machines exist and are not being used. Fiscal and monetary expansion can close that gap, which is a real achievement and the reason both instruments exist. What neither can do is push production past the boundary for long, because once the economy is on it the extra spending meets a fixed quantity of resources and raises prices instead. That is what Figure {fig:f31} shows, and it is the main limit on demand management. The one qualification is hysteresis, in section {sec:s24}: a long stay inside the frontier can pull the frontier itself inward, so closing a gap quickly protects capacity as well as using it.

Two things relax the constraint the frontier imposes. **Innovation**, in section {sec:s24}, raises what a given quantity of resources can produce: better technology, better skills, better organisation. It is the only source of growth in output per head that does not eventually run into the diminishing returns of section {sec:s2}, because every other route adds more of a factor to a fixed stock of the others. **Trade**, in sections {sec:s26} and {sec:s27}, does not change what a country can produce at all, and still lets it consume outside its own frontier. Specialising by comparative advantage and then exchanging is, in its effect, indistinguishable from acquiring a better technology for turning loaves into shirts.

Where on the frontier an economy ends up, and who receives what it produces, are separate questions, and section {sec:sineq} showed that economics can measure the answers but not choose between them. Scarcity forces choice, choice at the margin decides what gets made and who gets it, and the only ways out of the constraint are to use resources better or to trade with someone who uses them differently.
