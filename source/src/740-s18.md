## Aggregate supply {#s18}

> **DEFINITIONS.**\
> **Short-run aggregate supply**: the relationship between the price level and output while money wages and other input prices are fixed.\
> **Long-run aggregate supply**: the output the economy can sustain once every price and wage has adjusted, which depends on factors and productivity and not on the price level at all.\
> **Full employment output**: the level of output consistent with stable inflation, which is where long-run aggregate supply sits.\
> **Real wage**: the money wage divided by the price level, $w/P$, which measures what a wage buys.

Short-run aggregate supply comes straight from section {sec:s3}. A price-taking firm produces where $P = MC$, and ({eq:e12}) showed that $MC = w/MP_{L}$, where $w$ is the money wage. Across the whole economy, with the capital stock fixed in the short run,

$$P = \frac{w}{MP_{L}(L)},\quad\quad Y = F(K_{0}, L)\quad(\#esras)$$

Hold the money wage fixed, as it is in the short run by contract, by convention, or because expectations have not yet caught up. To produce more output firms must employ more labour; diminishing returns lower the marginal product of that labour; and with $w$ unchanged, a lower $MP_{L}$ means a higher marginal cost, which firms will cover only at a higher price. $SRAS$ slopes upward because of diminishing returns at a fixed money wage, the same mechanism that made the firm's marginal cost curve slope upward, now summed across firms.

Long-run aggregate supply removes the fixed money wage. Rearranged, ({eq:esras}) says $w/P = MP_{L}(L)$: firms employ labour until the real wage equals its marginal product, which is the labour demand condition of section {sec:slab}. Workers, for their part, decide how much to work by what a wage buys, so labour supply also depends on $w/P$. If the price level doubles and money wages double with it, the real wage is unchanged, so neither firms' demand for labour nor workers' supply of it has changed, and employment and output return to where they were. Long-run output is set where real labour demand meets real labour supply, given the capital stock and technology. None of that depends on the price level, so $LRAS$ is vertical at the full-employment level $Y_{FE}$.

$LRAS$ shifts only when the productive capacity of the economy changes: the size and skills of the labour force, the capital stock, technology, and the institutions and incentives that determine how well resources are allocated. This is the same event as an outward shift of the production possibility frontier in section {sec:s15}, drawn on different axes.

The **Keynesian** $AS$ curve is a third position: flat while there is mass unemployment, because idle workers and machines can be brought back without bidding up wages or costs, then rising as bottlenecks appear, then vertical at full capacity. Which shape is assumed dictates the answer about the effect of demand policy, so state the assumption rather than letting the diagram make the argument.

> **ASSUMPTIONS.**\
> $SRAS$: money wages fixed, diminishing returns to labour, capital and technology fixed.\
> $LRAS$: all prices and wages fully flexible, so that the real wage, and with it employment, is set in the labour market.\
> Firms are treated as price takers; with mark-up pricing the short-run curve slopes upward for the same reason, since the mark-up is applied to a marginal cost that rises with output.
