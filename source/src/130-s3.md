## Costs in the short run {#s3}

> **DEFINITIONS.**\
> **Fixed cost**: a cost that does not vary with output.\
> **Variable cost**: a cost that does.\
> **Total cost**: the two added together.\
> **Marginal cost**: the addition to total cost from making one more unit.\
> **Average total cost**: total cost per unit, equal to average fixed cost plus average variable cost.\
> **Average fixed cost**: fixed cost divided by output, which falls continuously and never turns back up.

Costs are technology valued at input prices. With the wage $w$ and the rental price of capital $R$ both given to the firm, and capital fixed at $K_{0}$,

$$TC(Q) = TFC + TVC = RK_{0} + wL(Q)\quad(\#e11)$$

**Total fixed cost** $TFC$ is the bill for capital, $RK_{0}$, the same whether output is zero or enormous. **Total variable cost** $TVC$ is the wage bill $wL(Q)$, where $L(Q)$ is the number of workers needed to make $Q$ units: the production function read backwards. Differentiate total cost with respect to output:

$$MC = \frac{dTC}{dQ} = w\,\frac{dL}{dQ} = \frac{w}{MP_{L}},\quad\quad AVC = \frac{wL}{Q} = \frac{w}{AP_{L}}\quad(\#e12)$$

The only way to make one more unit is to hire more labour, so the chain rule splits its cost into the wage and the extra labour each unit requires, $dL/dQ$. That last quantity is the reciprocal of the marginal product: if a worker adds 40 loaves, a loaf needs one fortieth of a worker. **Average variable cost** follows the same logic for the whole workforce rather than the last hire.

Marginal cost is the wage divided by marginal product, so **the shape of the cost curves is the shape of the product curves upside down**. While $MP_{L}$ is rising, $MC$ is falling; once diminishing marginal returns set in and $MP_{L}$ falls, $MC$ rises. The U-shape of $MC$ is derived from technology, not assumed. In the bakery: when each extra baker adds 40 loaves, the marginal cost of a loaf is $w/40$; when congestion around the oven cuts that to 10 loaves, the same wage buys a quarter as much output and marginal cost quadruples.

Dividing ({eq:e11}) through by $Q$ splits average total cost into two components that behave quite differently:

$$ATC = \frac{TFC}{Q} + \frac{TVC}{Q} = AFC + AVC\quad(\#e13)$$

$AFC$ falls continuously towards zero, because a fixed number is being divided by a rising one, and it never turns up. $AVC$ is U-shaped for the reason just given. And ({eq:e10}) applies to costs as it did to products: average cost falls while marginal cost is below it and rises while marginal cost is above it. So $MC$ passes through the minimum of $ATC$, and by the same argument through the minimum of $AVC$.

Three separate forces move unit costs, and they are constantly confused with one another:

  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                                 What happens           Why                                            When it applies
  ------------------------------ ---------------------- ---------------------------------------------- -----------------------------------------
  Spreading fixed costs          $AFC$ falls            a fixed number divided by a rising one         every output, and it never reverses

  Diminishing marginal returns   $MC$ then $AVC$ rise   a fixed factor, so $MP_{L}$ eventually falls   short run only

  Economies of scale             $LRAC$ falls           a larger plant is cheaper per unit             long run only, when every factor varies
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Writing "economies of scale" on a short-run diagram, or explaining falling $ATC$ by economies of scale when it is really $AFC$ being spread, is the most common error in this part of the course.

> **ASSUMPTIONS.**\
> The firm buys labour at a given wage and rents capital at a given price, so it is too small to move either.\
> Capital is fixed, which is what makes this the short run and what makes diminishing returns possible at all.\
> Every cost is an economic cost, including the opportunity cost of the owner's own time and money, which is why these curves are not an accountant's cost curves.
