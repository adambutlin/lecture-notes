## Equilibrium, output gaps and the cycle {#s19}

> **DEFINITIONS.**\
> **Macroeconomic equilibrium**: the price level and output at which aggregate demand equals aggregate supply.\
> **Output gap**: actual output minus the sustainable level, negative when there is spare capacity and positive when the economy is overheating.\
> **Business cycle**: the movement of that gap through boom, slowdown, recession and recovery.\
> **Trend growth**: the rate at which sustainable output grows over time.\
> **Recession**: conventionally, two consecutive quarters of falling real GDP.

Equilibrium is where $AD = SRAS$, giving a price level and a level of real output together. The output gap measures how far that output sits from what the economy could sustain with its existing factors and technology:

$$\text{output gap} = Y - Y_{FE}\quad(\#e46)$$

![Figure {fig:f26}. Macroeconomic equilibrium with a negative and a positive output gap.](figs/f26.png)

A **negative** gap means production below capacity: unemployment above its natural rate, spare capacity, and downward pressure on inflation. A **positive** gap means the economy is producing beyond its sustainable level by working existing factors unusually hard, with labour shortages, bidding up of wages and rising inflation. Over time, $Y_{FE}$ itself grows as $LRAS$ shifts out, and the business cycle is the movement of actual output around that rising trend: a boom when output runs above it, a recession when output falls, and a recovery as the gap closes.

![Figure {fig:cycle}. The business cycle. Actual output fluctuates around a rising trend of sustainable output, and the output gap is the vertical distance between them.](figs/cycle.png)

Left alone, the economy closes a negative gap by itself, and ({eq:esras}) shows how. Output below capacity raises unemployment, workers' bargaining power weakens, and money wage growth slows. A lower $w$ lowers $w/MP_{L}$ at every level of output, so $SRAS$ moves down, the price level falls, and the movement down the AD curve raises output back towards $Y_{FE}$.

> **ASSUMPTIONS.**\
> Self-correction depends on money wages actually falling, or at least rising more slowly than prices.\
> Keynes's objection was not that this mechanism is wrong in theory but that it can take years, and that the unemployment in the meantime is real. That disagreement, about the speed of wage adjustment, is most of the difference between classical and Keynesian policy conclusions.\
> The trend is drawn as smooth. Section {sec:s24} explains why a long recession can lower the trend itself.
